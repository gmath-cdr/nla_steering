What transferred when concept steering met fresh prompts?

A prospectively frozen, curated ten-concept panel tested visible prompting and three hidden-state methods on Qwen and Gemma. The answer is not a simple NLA win: direct prompting was strongest, DiffMean had the highest hidden-method point estimate, and NLA showed genuine but uneven transfer.

What each term means

Carrier prompt

The ordinary user request answered by the model. It does not ask for the target concept.

Concept

The content or style we try to introduce into an otherwise unrelated answer.

Calibration

Separate prompts used only to choose α. Their outputs are not counted as held-out evidence.

α (alpha)

The preselected strength of a hidden-state direction. One value was frozen per concept, method, and model.

AxBench Prompt

A visible instruction asking for the concept. It is a practical ceiling/reference, not hidden steering.

Controls

Unsteered output and three matched random directions show what happens without a meaningful target direction.

AxBench harmonic

A 0–2 combined score balancing concept presence, instruction adherence, and fluency.

Prompt-specific NLA

The AV verbalizes the carrier state, the concept sentence is appended, and AR reconstructs a carrier-specific direction.

Fail-closed

Any hash, count, cell, checkpoint, receipt, or judge mismatch stops release instead of silently continuing.

Overall scores

Visible prompting dominates; hidden steering varies

Quality-aware metric

Concept gain is not enough by itself

The harmonic score falls when a method mentions the concept but damages instruction adherence or fluency. DiffMean produced the strongest hidden-method concept score, but also the highest target-model perplexity: 14.8 on Qwen and 36.7 on Gemma.

Preregistered comparisons

The central claim is mixed, not a ranking victory

Qwen: NLA trailed DiffMean by 0.18; the interval crossed zero. NLA exceeded CAA by 0.27, but Holm-adjusted p=0.050 is just above .05. Gemma: neither NLA comparison separated clearly from zero.

NLA did beat each of the three matched random controls on both models in descriptive paired analyses. That supports direction-specific signal; it does not establish NLA as the best method.

Generalization view

Where the prompt came from mattered

The frozen design used two held-out tracks: source-negative Concept500 prompts specific to each concept, and the same shared open-ended Alpaca prompts across concepts. We report them separately and do not treat either small track as a standalone confirmatory result.

Concept-level anatomy

No method worked uniformly across concepts

Do not rank concepts from this alone. Each concept has only eight held-out cells (two prompts per track × two seeds). These heatmaps are hypothesis-generating.

Calibration receipt

Strength was chosen before evaluation

Calibration searched the frozen grid, applied the frozen rule, and sealed one α for every concept/method/model. Evaluation then used only that chosen α; it did not search the held-out responses.

What this closes—and what it does not

Claim boundary

Supported

  • Visible AxBench prompting was strongest.
  • DiffMean and NLA both carried concept signal beyond random controls.
  • Performance changed substantially by concept and prompt track.

Not supported

  • No general NLA-over-DiffMean victory.
  • No arbitrary-concept generality claim.
  • No per-concept significance claims.
  • No J-space or JLens conclusion.

Design limits

  • Curated, outcome-blind panel—not random Concept500.
  • Two held-out prompts per track.
  • Injection schedules differ by historical method.
  • One exact judge model and fixed decoding protocol.
