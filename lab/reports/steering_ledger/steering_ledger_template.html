Current-method status (2026-08-19). This ledger was originally written as a chronological narrative. The audited taxonomy now controls scientific labels. Authored AR text pairs are not AV-edit-AR; J-lens readout is a diagnostic, not a root vector construction; and the greedy-NNLS k25 remainder is not known to be orthogonal, globally non-J, null, or causally unnecessary. Method 1 · hand-written pairs

Where it all started: write two sentences, subtract

Qwen: the original authored AR emoji contrast, pooled to 30 replies per point across two independently seeded waves. Emojis rise from zero through the 0.4–0.8 window; random stays flat. On Gemma the same texts, rebuilt in Gemma’s residual space, act inside its 0.05–0.2 range. This is a cross-model descriptive replication; the data do not establish that only the alpha dial differs.

Method 2 · one-word edits

Change one word in the model’s own self-description

Exploratory observation. On Qwen wave 1, inserting excitement words such as “excited” and “cheerful” produced no emoji effect, while some arms increased exclamation marks. Their J-lens readouts were inspected alongside these outcomes. This is useful hypothesis-generating alignment, not a blinded forecast test.

Qwen: observed exclamation outcomes for excitement-word AV-edit-AR arms, shown with the readouts that were interpreted retrospectively. One arm whose readout lacked “!” stayed flat; the random arm declined.

The corrected design on Qwen (left) and Gemma (right): all five target-naming words steer on both models. Open circles mark emoji floods where language collapses; one-word vectors are potent but nearly impossible to dose gently. Note the shuffle: “smiley” is Gemma’s strongest and was Qwen’s weakest.

Method 3 · phrase edits

Phrase-level AV-edit-AR in the emoji setting

Left, Qwen: all five phrase rewrites of the exact AV substrate produced large emoji effects; the literal-glyph arm was strongest in this campaign. Derived analysis · J-lens readout and sparse approximation

The greedy-NNLS k25 component and its remainder

The split, precisely. Greedy sparse nonnegative coding selects at most 25 unit-L2 J-lens atoms and refits nonnegative weights to approximate a named parent vector. The resulting k25 component is a solver- and dictionary-relative approximation. The k25 approximation remainder is parent minus that component. It is not guaranteed orthogonal to the dictionary, outside global J-space, null, dead, or unable to route through J coordinates downstream. The archived interventions independently unit-normalize parent, component, and remainder; comparisons therefore measure potency per norm, not each piece’s native contribution. The implementation is reference-inspired and not the paper’s unpublished gradient-pursuit solver.

Each panel shows a unit-normalized parent (blue), its independently unit-normalized k25 component (green), and independently unit-normalized approximation remainder (gray). Parents are the authored AR emoji contrast and the AV-edit-AR glyph phrase. On Qwen, several components act at lower alpha while multiple remainders remain active. On Gemma, selected remainders are often quieter, but the glyph-phrase remainder reaches a substantial emoji effect at alpha 0.2. These parent-specific results motivate a localization hypothesis; they do not establish a universal model-level property.

The wordle campaign · the program’s hardest problem, start to finish

From “it always says CRANE” to HOUSE

The problem. Ask either model for a wordle opening word and it answers CRANE, over and over. The question: can a vector injected at the middle layer move that answer to a word of our choosing, while the prompt never changes?

The prompts, verbatim

“Think of a word to start a wordle game.” “Give me a starting word for wordle.” “What’s a good wordle opener?” “Suggest a wordle starting word.” “Five letter word for Wordle” the pointed fifth prompt carried the first experiment; the natural four carried the replication. Nothing else was ever asked.
model, unsteeredfavorite answerhow often
Qwen2.5-7BCRANE92% under the pointed prompt · 48% under the natural four (ADIEU second)
Gemma-3-12BCRANE35% (ADIEU 15%)
The mission. Keep the prompts frozen and make the model answer HOUSE. That takes two things at once: push the meaning of the answer toward dwellings, and keep the five-letter format the game demands. Everything so far said those two travel very differently.

The prompt never changes; only the injection does. The vector is two plain sentences, reconstructed and subtracted, and neither sentence contains house, home, or crane. Only the meaning of a dwelling crosses. Qwen delivers that meaning in its own four-letter word; Gemma finds the one word that satisfies meaning and format together.
